Glenroe-Ballyorgan AR App

An iOS app that preserves the history and culture of Glenroe-Ballyorgan, a rural community in the Ballyhoura region of Ireland, through GPS-triggered audio tours, an AR bird-finding river walk and community content.

Overview

Much of Glenroe-Ballyorgan's heritage lived on paper, on signs along the trails, or on a Living Land audio-tour CD sold only at tourist offices. Working with Ballyhoura Development, I reviewed the existing digital efforts in the area, gathered input from the community and turned the findings into twelve functional requirements for one app that both younger residents and visitors would use.

The result is a native iOS app built on an MVC architecture. The audio tour brings all 22 tracks from the CD to the places they describe. On the Keale River walk, animated AR birds appear at the real signposts that introduce each species. There are also local history articles, community events and comments. Firebase handles accounts, media and real-time data, Mapbox draws the routes, and ARKit with SceneKit renders the birds.

Architecture

Model-View-Controller, as in the thesis. Controllers coordinate the device sensors and Firebase, models stay reusable, and views only render.

Key features

GPS-triggered audio tours

Every stop on the tour is a 50 m CLCircularRegion monitored by CLLocationManager. Entering a region plays its story through AVAudioPlayer, and the app tracks the active region so one stop can't retrigger while you linger.

Audio is downloaded once from Firebase Storage, with a progress bar, and cached on the device, so the tour still works where rural mobile coverage drops out.

AR bird-finding walk along the River Keale

As you walk, locationManager(_:didUpdateLocations:) checks your distance to each bird location. Within 30 m, the matching SceneKit model (a robin, a bluebird or the local barn owl) is placed in an ARWorldTrackingConfiguration session and follows the camera.

The trigger points match the physical signs along the walk that introduce each species, so the AR layer adds to the real trail instead of replacing it.

Talk to the birds

During the AR walk you can ask questions out loud. SFSpeechRecognizer streams microphone buffers, and a Levenshtein-distance fuzzy matcher maps what it heard to the nearest known question, so "what's special about this place" still gets the answer about the Keale River's ancient woodland.

Strict command matching was too brittle in testing because of wind, accents and different microphones, so it was replaced. A 2-second silence timer stops the recogniser to save power and avoid processing background noise forever.

Animated routes, events and community comments

Tour routes are GeoJSON files drawn with Mapbox, and the polyline grows point by point to show the way. Recent events come from a JSON file in Firebase Storage, so the community can update them by replacing a file, with no database changes or app release.

History articles on the Explore tab have a comment section backed by Firebase Realtime Database, which syncs new comments to every reader instantly.

Comments without clutter

The comment area stays hidden until a reader asks for it, which keeps articles clean. Signing in with email or Google through Firebase Auth is needed only to post.

A fix I'm pleased with: the keyboard used to cover the text field. The view now listens for keyboard notifications, adjusts the scroll view's insets and scrolls the field into view.

Design

The icon brings together a tree, the river and a bird, which stand for the AR bird walk along the River Keale. The palette is black, white and soft greens, with yellow and orange accents taken from Ballyhoura sunsets. The type is AvenirNext alongside the system font. The app opens on Interactive Tours, its main feature, and a bottom tab bar gives one-tap access to tours, events, Explore and the user's account.

Key design decisions from the thesis:

Decision Chosen Rejected because
Data Firebase (Auth, Storage, Realtime DB) Core Data has no built-in cloud sync or multi-user access
Maps Mapbox + MapKit Apple Maps alone lacked GeoJSON overlays and custom styling
AR ARKit + SceneKit Unity AR Foundation was too heavy for an iOS-only app
Voice SFSpeechRecognizer + fuzzy matching Exact command matching failed on real speech

Testing and evaluation

Apple's testing restrictions limited how much user feedback I could collect, so the evaluation relied on XCTest suites, an iPhone XS on site and Instruments profiling:

  • Unit tests covered AR session setup and model placement, all eight VoiceControlManager cases (including Levenshtein matching and the silence timer) and the audio tour's location manager and geofences. The test for entering a region was hard to automate without real GPS and remote files, and the thesis discusses that limitation openly.
  • Integration tests checked GPS → UI updates, voice → audio playback, and comments round-tripping through Firebase in real time. All passed.
  • Profiling with Instruments (Core Animation, Time Profiler and memory analysis) showed a responsive UI. It also flagged CPU and heat spikes during AR rendering and audio downloads, plus memory growth worth chasing, and those became the optimisation backlog.

Challenges

  • Animating a GeoJSON route. Static rendering loads every coordinate at once, but the animation needed them one at a time. I parsed the GeoJSON by hand and fed a timer that updates the Mapbox source every 100 ms.
  • A recogniser that never stopped. Background noise kept speech recognition alive. The fix was the silence timer, which resets whenever new words arrive.
  • Dependency conflicts between the Mapbox and Firebase packages turned out to be a typo in .netrc, found by working through network and configuration checks one by one.

What's next

Gesture and richer voice interaction in AR, community-contributed stories and photos pinned to locations, and more local species and tours as more of the area's heritage is digitised.
